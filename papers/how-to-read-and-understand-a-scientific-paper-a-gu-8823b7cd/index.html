How to read and understand a scientific paper: a guide for non-scientists

  • Authors:
  • Jennifer Raff
Read the paper
Published: August 25, 2013; updated January 3, 2018

📜 Abstract

No formal abstract or dedicated summary section is provided on the HTML page. The article instead introduces itself as a guide intended to help people without a science or medicine background read primary research papers, understand their methods and results, and assess whether the studies are reputable.

✨ Summary

The article presents an eleven-step method for reading primary scientific research. Its central recommendations are to begin with the introduction rather than the abstract, identify the field’s broad question and the study’s specific questions, understand the proposed approach and methods, examine results independently of the authors’ interpretation, evaluate limitations and alternative explanations, and consult other researchers’ views only after forming an initial assessment. It also emphasizes looking up unfamiliar terminology, taking notes, interpreting figures and tables, and distinguishing primary research articles from review articles.

The guide has had documented educational and science-communication uptake. It was republished by the London School of Economics in 2016 as a resource for non-scientists and academic readers. (blogs.lse.ac.uk) Carnegie Mellon University’s Master of Science in Learning Engineering program recommends it as a guide for analyzing research papers and specifically highlights its emphasis on note-taking and reflection. (msle.hcii.cmu.edu) Adapted versions have been distributed as research-literacy handouts by educational institutions, including the College of DuPage. (codlrc.org) The guide is also listed in university course materials and cited as a resource in science-literacy instruction, including a multistep biology classroom framework. (sciencedirect.com) These sources indicate influence primarily through teaching, course design, and public-facing research-literacy materials rather than through a formal research-citation tradition.
